Mini Games

Six small games built around things I actually work with: flying drones, reading measuring tools, tuning control loops, gearing a motor, reading technical drawings and wiring logic. They run in your browser, and your best scores are kept on this device only.

  • Soft Landing

Soft Landing

Bring the drone down onto the pad gently enough not to break it, before the battery runs out. Every level the wind picks up and the pad gets narrower.

Keyboard: ↑, W or Space for thrust, ← → or A D to drift, after clicking the screen. Touch: hold the buttons under it.

Read the Caliper

Ten readings against the clock. Take the last main-scale line before the vernier’s zero, then add the vernier line that lines up exactly with a line on the main scale.

Type the reading in millimetres and press Enter. Zoom in when the lines are hard to tell apart: a real one is often read under a loupe too.

Tune the Loop

Set the three gains of a PID controller so the system reaches its target quickly, without overshooting too far, and stays there. Four systems, each needing something different.

Drag the sliders and the response redraws as you go. Meeting every target earns a star; settling faster earns the second and third.

Gear Train

The motor turns at 1200 rpm clockwise. Pick the gears so the output shaft turns at the target speed and in the target direction, with as few gears as you can.

Each stage is one gear driving another; an idler between them keeps the direction. Up to three stages, with gears of 12 to 60 teeth. The animation runs slower than life so the teeth can be followed.

    Three Views

    A block of cubes, drawn in perspective. Pick the drawing that is its front, top or side view, as it would appear on an engineering drawing.

    Dashed lines are edges hidden behind the block. The top view has the front of the block at the bottom of the sheet. Eight questions, from small blocks to larger ones; keys A to D also answer.

    Light the LED

    Fill each empty slot with a logic gate so the LED follows the rule, in seven small circuits taken from real machines, from a safety press to a drone’s arming logic.

    Pick a gate from each list, or click the gate in the drawing to cycle through them. Flip the switches to try a case; the table checks every case at once. Symbols are IEC: & is AND, ≥1 is OR, =1 is XOR, and a circle inverts.
